# Scroll journey

**The governing principle: paint the stops, film the passages.**

- A **stop** is a painted still that stands alone. It is where the reader rests and reads. It is also the poster, the reduced-motion image, the no-script image and the fallback when a clip fails. So it must be perfect and permanent, and a still is both.
- A **passage** is a short silent clip that carries the camera from one stop to the next. It only has to be good while it moves. Because both of its ends are pinned to painted stills, the joins disappear.
- **The words are HTML**, laid over the painting's calm side when the page is built. No words, logos or lettering are ever baked into a still or a clip.

The reader's scroll is the playhead. Holds rest on stills; passages scrub through clips; scrolling back plays them in reverse.

## When to use it, and when not

| You want | Use |
|---|---|
| A section where scrolling travels through a painted world, resting at stops with copy | **this skill** |
| A short silent banner that loops in place | `scripts/loop_seal.py` (see `references/pipeline.md`), not the journey |
| A narrated or scored video with a play button | a video workflow, not this skill |
| Clips generated by a paid API from a script | not this route: it is manual by design, so it needs no keys |

## What the person and their machine need

- **The human:** approves three gates (the board, the look, the render go), paints the stills with any image tool that can paint at 16:9 and 9:16, and renders each passage by hand in Google Flow. Flow, its Frames to Video mode and its video model are a Google web product reached with the human's own Google account; plans, credits, limits and names change, so treat every Flow fact in this skill as "(as of October 2026; re-check before relying on it)".
- **The machine:** `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` 5.1 or newer, Python 3.9+ with `numpy` and `Pillow`, CPU only. For the site: an Astro project (the template was built and checked against Astro 7). For the phone battery: Node with Playwright installed in the site folder.

Check the toolkit once, before the first job. Every script carries a self-test with a positive control (a case that must fail). `SJ` is this skill's `scripts` folder: once installed it is `$HOME/.claude/skills/scroll-journey/scripts`; before installing, point it at `03_skills/scroll-journey/scripts` inside the unzipped package.

```bash
SJ="$HOME/.claude/skills/scroll-journey/scripts"   # the installed skill; from the unzipped package use 03_skills/scroll-journey/scripts
ffmpeg -version | head -1
python3 -c "import numpy, PIL; print('numpy', numpy.__version__, 'Pillow', PIL.__version__)"
mkdir -p ~/.cache/scroll-journey/logs
for t in boundary_frames seam_check color_match web_normalize seam_repair route_sheet verify_web_media loop_seal freeze_detect blend_detect continuity_audit contact_sheet stop_check placeholder_stills; do python3 -B "$SJ/$t.py" --selftest > ~/.cache/scroll-journey/logs/selftest_$t.log 2>&1; echo "$t rc=$?"; done
```

Every line must read `rc=0` (the self-tests take a few minutes on a laptop; scratch goes under `~/.cache/scroll-journey/`, or wherever `SCROLL_JOURNEY_SCRATCH` points). A failing test's reason is the last line of its log. If `web_normalize` reports `webm=skipped` or `av1=skipped`, that ffmpeg build lacks the optional encoder; the H.264 files the site needs are unaffected.

## The route, step by step

Keep everything for one journey in one **package folder** outside the site repo (for example `~/journeys/<slug>/`):

```text
<package>/
  BRIEF.md          step 0: audience, the one idea, the peak stop, the copy
  BOARD.md          step 1: stops and passages, one table each
  ingredients/      the exact stills the human uploads to Flow (S0_16x9.png, S0_9x16.png, ...)
  HANDOFF.md        step 3: the passage prompts, written for the human
  renders/          the human's raw Flow downloads: P1_16x9_A_take1.mp4 ...
  work/             gate reports, contact sheets, intermediates (never shipped)
  web/              the final encodes, named for the site: p1-16x9-v1.mp4 ...
  STATUS.md         the ledger: approved, painted, rendered, gated, encoded, built, shipped, with every measured number
```

**0. Brief.** One page: the audience, the one idea the journey must leave behind, the page's already-approved copy for each stop (read it fresh from the source, never retype from memory), and **the peak**. The peak of a journey is an *arrival at a stop*, not a passage: readers remember where they arrived. Give the peak stop the longest hold and the most deliberate approach. Length is measured in **scroll** (screen heights), not seconds.

**1. Board, then PAUSE.** Write `BOARD.md`. Per stop: ID and name, the painting (both aspects), the calm side for the words, the focal point, the hold length, the words, and any door (a link over a painted form). Per passage: ID, start and end stop, the camera move, the travel medium, the aspects (16:9 and 9:16), the scroll length. Alternate the words' side (left, right, left, ...) so each wide passage carries a gentle sideways drift. **The human approves the board before anything is painted.**

**2. Paint the stops, then PAUSE.** Read `references/stills.md` (and `references/white_ground.md` for a white page). Paint two or three style samples first (normally the first, a middle and the last stop); **the human approves the look**; then paint the rest. Measure each stop's words side with `stop_check.py`. Byte-copy the chosen masters into `ingredients/` and never re-export them.

**3. Write the passages.** Read `references/omni_passages.md`. Write `HANDOFF.md`: per passage and aspect, the two ingredient paths, the five-part prompt, take A in full and take B as a replacement MOTION paragraph, the length, the aspect and the download filename. Give every passage its own choreography and keep the uniqueness ledger. Then **audit the prompts with fresh eyes** (ideally a separate subagent given only the handoff, the board and the stills, not your reasoning) against the checklist in `references/qa_checklist.md` part A. Fix every finding before the human spends a render.

**4. Probe first.** The human renders **one** passage (the hardest, at 16:9, take A) and stops. Gate it within minutes (step 6). The probe answers what nothing else can: does the model land on the end frame, what length and size it really returns, whether it adds audio, and whether the motion reads as flight. If the probe misses, fix the prompt doctrine across the whole handoff, not only that passage, and probe again.

**5. Render handoff, then PAUSE.** Only with the human's explicit go for the batch, the human renders the rest and saves each download into `renders/` as `<PromptID>_<aspect>_<take>_take<N>.mp4` (for example `P2_9x16_A_take1.mp4`). If they name files their own way, map the names; never block on naming.

**6. Render gate.** Every raw take gets measured and looked at before anything is encoded: seam SSIM at both ends against the stills, colour drift, hidden cuts, freezes, soft dissolves, the paper (white pages), no new objects or lettering, and a 2-frames-per-second contact sheet read by eye. Verdict per take: **PASS**, **REPAIRABLE** (a mechanical fix restores it) or **RE-RENDER** (change one thing in the prompt, re-audit, the human renders a fresh take). Three failures for the same reason on one passage: stop and bring the human the takes, the numbers and the diagnosis. Details: `references/pipeline.md` § The render gate.

**7. Mechanical pass.** Run `references/pipeline.md` in its exact order: paper lift (white pages), seam repair at both ends, web encodes for every tier, seam check again, verify, route sheet. This pass is mechanical by definition. Anything that needs taste goes back to the board or to a re-render, never into this pass.

**8. Build.** Read `references/build_astro.md`. Copy the template into the site, write the journey's data file, put the stills in `src/assets/journeys/<slug>/` and the encodes in `public/video/<slug>/`, and theme it with CSS custom properties.

**9. QA.** Run `references/qa_checklist.md` part B on the built page, including the five-size phone battery in `references/mobile.md`, then ask the human for a look on a **real phone** before calling mobile done. An emulator has no browser bars.

**10. Record.** Write every measured number and every lesson into `STATUS.md` while it is fresh. If you have a memory tool, store the lessons there too; the next journey starts from them.

## Hard rules

- **Stops are stills; passages are clips.** A stop never needs a clip.
- **Silent, wordless clips.** The copy is HTML. The video model may add an audio track even when told "Silent"; the encoder strips it (`-an`) every time.
- **No key, no API, no `.env`.** The human renders by hand. Never add a video API call to this route.
- **Probe before the batch.** One render tests the whole prompt doctrine; a batch written on an untested doctrine wastes every render.
- **Measure, then look, then judge.** A number decides nothing until the two frames have been read side by side; when a metric passes and the picture is wrong, believe the picture. The eye catches defects that every measurement passes, such as an invented shape mid-flight or faint tiling lines.
- **One change per re-render.** The human's renders cost their time and credits.
- **Never import clips through the bundler.** They live in `public/` under versioned names and are served with byte ranges.
- **Every gate the human owns stays theirs.** Do not infer approval from silence or from an earlier general instruction.
