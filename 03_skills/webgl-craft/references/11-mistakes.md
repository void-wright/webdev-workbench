## 11. The ten biggest WebGL mistakes to avoid

1. **Measuring fps in headless Chrome without checking the renderer.** Headless runs may use SwiftShader, a software renderer, so their results may not represent hardware rendering. Check the renderer string and record the test environment before interpreting frame rates.
2. **Booting the GPU on load (or on scroll).** Defer decorative interactive layers until first interaction, or use a deliberate visibility policy after page load and idle time. Starting on `DOMContentLoaded` competes with initial page work; Astro `client:visible` triggers when a component enters the viewport, which does not prove that the visitor wants animation.
3. **Revealing the canvas before a frame is proven.** Premature visibility can cause black flashes and abrupt transitions. Make frame 0 match the poster, confirm a successful draw, wait through two `requestAnimationFrame` callbacks, then fade in over roughly 200 ms. Adding `.live` immediately after scene construction does not prove that anything rendered.
4. **Uncapped DPR and MSAA.** Rendering at unrestricted device pixel ratios can multiply pixel work, while multisample anti-aliasing adds further cost. Cap DPR and choose anti-aliasing deliberately. Check their combined effect before adding visual complexity.
5. **Default square points and live per-node blur.** `PointsMaterial` without a suitable map draws squares; live SVG `feGaussianBlur` on individual nodes can trigger expensive repainting during hover. Use reusable halo textures or another bounded glow technique, and check that the static fallback and interactive rendering share the intended appearance.
6. **Frame-rate-dependent motion and `setInterval` loops.** Fixed movement per frame runs faster on higher-refresh displays, while timer-driven rendering can do work that never reaches the screen. Use `requestAnimationFrame` with elapsed-time-based motion. Reset the clock after a pause so re-entry does not produce a sudden jump.
7. **No reduced-motion path and no pause control.** Respect reduced-motion preferences and provide a static alternative. For automatically starting motion that lasts more than five seconds alongside other content, provide a visible pause, stop or hide control as required by WCAG 2.2.2, unless the motion is essential. Reduced-motion support alone does not replace that control.
8. **Trapping the page scroll.** Calling `preventDefault` on touch events can block normal scrolling. For an interactive layer that allows vertical page movement, use `touch-action: pan-y` and keep gesture handling scoped to the interaction that needs it.
9. **Putting meaning where it cannot be read.** Keep meaningful text in accessible HTML rather than only in the canvas. Do not make a dimmed line or star the sole carrier of meaning. Check the final composited colours against the actual background, including hover and dimmed states; required graphical objects need 3:1 contrast unless an applicable exception applies. Add readable labels or other cues.
10. **Importing an engine from a CDN, or trusting unverified numbers.** An external engine script may conflict with a strict CSP and exceed the page's byte budget. Bundle a pinned dependency when appropriate, measure the delivered bytes, and check its current licence and browser support. Verify claims about WebGPU usage and loading metrics rather than assuming a canvas improves LCP.

Honourable mentions: synchronous GPU `readPixels` picking can stall rendering; missing context-loss handling leaves a broken layer; unseeded `Math.random` or different PRNGs across build and browser code break poster parity; mismatched `outputColorSpace` settings distort colours; accumulated motion drifts where periodic displacement would close a loop; component periods must align with the intended loop duration; half-float stateful positions can lose precision; an accent colour spread across the whole scene loses its emphasis; colour literals in scripts drift from shared design tokens.
